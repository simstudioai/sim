## TL;DR

[Tines](https://www.tines.com/) remains a relevant choice for teams that want secure, governed automation. Teams building agents for marketing, sales, finance, support, research, and internal operations should also evaluate these six alternatives:

- **Sim** fits teams that need flexible AI agents, broad model choice, and customer-managed deployment.
- **n8n** fits technical teams that want [workflow automation, AI capabilities, and self-hosting](https://docs.n8n.io/deploy/host-n8n/deploy-with-the-ai-starter-kit).
- **Workato** fits enterprises that prioritize [governed integration across business systems](https://www.workato.com/agentstudio).
- **Zapier** fits business teams that want [hosted automation across a broad app ecosystem](https://zapier.com/).
- **Make** fits teams that want [detailed visual automation with credit-based usage](https://www.make.com/en/pricing).
- **Gumloop** fits teams building [hosted AI workflows for data and document tasks](https://docs.gumloop.com/core-concepts/workbooks).

The tools are not ordered by score. The right choice depends on departmental fit, agent requirements, deployment control, governance, and the billing unit that matches the expected workload.

## What is the best Tines alternative for teams outside security and IT operations?

Sim fits teams outside security and IT operations that need to build, deploy, and manage AI agents for multiple departments. It combines a visual workflow builder, model choice, application tools, human review, reusable workflows, and customer-managed deployment in the open-source AI workspace.

Workspace bring-your-own-key support is available on every Sim Cloud plan. Organization-level keys require Pro for Teams, Max for Teams, or Enterprise, as documented in [Sim's cost guide](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs#bring-your-own-key-byok). On self-hosted deployments, teams can connect local models through Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio, or LiteLLM using the [self-hosting guide](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/self-hosting).

Sim's core is licensed under [Apache 2.0](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE). Features in `apps/sim/ee`, including SSO, SCIM, audit logs, access control, and data retention, use the separate [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE). That license permits development, testing, and internal non-production use, while production use requires an active Sim Enterprise subscription.

For adjacent evaluation criteria, read [Best AI Agent Platforms and Builders in 2026](https://www.sim.ai/library/best-ai-agent-platforms-2026) and [What to Look for in an AI Workflow Automation Platform](https://www.sim.ai/library/ai-workflow-automation-platform-buyers-checklist).

## What are the key licensing and deployment distinctions?

Sim's core combines an OSI-approved Apache 2.0 license with documented customer-managed deployment and local model support. The enterprise exception matters: code in `apps/sim/ee` is governed by the Sim Enterprise License, not Apache 2.0.

n8n makes much of its source visible, but its [Sustainable Use License](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md) is source available rather than OSI-approved open source. Its license restricts some uses, including providing the software to others as a hosted or managed service.

Tines also now documents [self-hosted deployment](https://www.tines.com/blog/understanding-your-tines-deployment-options/), so deployment alone does not separate it from Sim or n8n. Buyers should compare licensing rights, supported infrastructure, upgrade responsibilities, model policy, and enterprise controls. [Apache 2.0 vs Fair Code](https://www.sim.ai/library/apache-2-0-vs-fair-code) explains the licensing distinction in more detail.

## Why consider Sim for flexible AI agents?

Sim is the open-source AI workspace where teams build, deploy, and manage AI agents. Its workflow builder combines model calls, application tools, APIs, conditions, parallel paths, human input, and reusable workflows. This approach fits work such as lead enrichment, customer onboarding, report generation, document processing, internal knowledge retrieval, and support triage.

Infrastructure teams can run Sim on customer-managed infrastructure and connect Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio, or LiteLLM. Teams evaluating that requirement can compare approaches in [What Are the Best Self Hosted AI Workflow Automation Platforms in 2026?](https://www.sim.ai/library/best-self-hosted-ai-workflow-automation-platforms-2026).

**Fits:** Teams that want general-purpose AI agents, visual building, model choice, and customer-managed deployment.

**Main tradeoff:** Advanced administration features in `apps/sim/ee` require an Enterprise subscription for production use.

## Why consider n8n for technical automation?

n8n combines application nodes, webhooks, code steps, AI integrations, and workflow execution controls. Its [AI tooling supports configurable agents, language models, memory, and other components](https://docs.n8n.io/build/integrate-ai/langchain-in-n8n), which can serve product, data, operations, and engineering teams.

The central licensing distinction is that n8n is source available under the Sustainable Use License, not OSI-approved open source. Teams comparing it with other options can read [12 Best n8n Alternatives and Competitors for AI Agents in 2026](https://www.sim.ai/library/n8n-alternatives).

**Fits:** Technical teams that want self-hosted workflow automation and code-friendly customization.

**Main tradeoff:** The Sustainable Use License restricts some commercial uses, including offering n8n as a hosted or managed service to others.

## Why consider Workato for enterprise integration?

Workato fits centralized enterprise programs that prioritize integration, administration, and governance across business systems. Its [Agent Studio](https://www.workato.com/agentstudio) supports enterprise agents, while its [pricing page](https://www.workato.com/pricing) describes platform editions with application integration, process automation, lifecycle management, and role-based controls.

**Fits:** Enterprises standardizing integration and automation across many departments.

**Main tradeoff:** Buyers need to map Workato's platform edition and usage model to their contract and expected workload.

## Why consider Zapier for business app automation?

Zapier fits business teams that prioritize familiar application automation and quick setup. Its product suite combines [Zaps, Agents, Tables, and Forms](https://zapier.com/) in a hosted service, making it suitable for marketing, sales, and operations processes.

**Fits:** Business teams that want accessible hosted automation across common software services.

**Main tradeoff:** Task-based billing requires workload forecasting as automation volume grows.

## Why consider Make for visual automation?

Make fits teams that want detailed visual scenario design without operating their own infrastructure. Its [visual product](https://www.make.com/en/product) combines branching workflows, application integrations, data transformation, and AI agents for business automation.

**Fits:** Teams that want visual control over hosted business automations.

**Main tradeoff:** Complex scenarios can make credit consumption harder to forecast because module actions consume credits.

## Why consider Gumloop for hosted AI workflows?

Gumloop fits teams that want hosted AI workflows for document, web, and data-intensive tasks. Its [workflows connect visual nodes](https://docs.gumloop.com/core-concepts/workbooks), while its agents can choose connected tools for open-ended tasks.

**Fits:** Teams that want a hosted visual product for AI-centered data and document workflows.

**Main tradeoff:** Teams that require customer-managed infrastructure should confirm deployment requirements before shortlisting it.

## How is Tines positioned in this market?

[Tines positions its product as a secure environment for enterprise AI](https://www.tines.com/). Its current product supports agents, apps, and automations for broad business use, and the company documents both cloud and self-hosted deployment.

That fit differs from Sim's focus on flexible AI agent creation across departments, n8n's technical workflow automation, Zapier and Make's hosted business automation, Workato's enterprise integration, and Gumloop's hosted AI tasks. A team's existing security operations investment may make Tines the practical choice even when another platform fits a new departmental use case more closely.

## Is Tines suitable for enterprise AI workflows?

Tines fits enterprise AI workflows when secure execution, governance, and controlled operations are central requirements. Its [AI product](https://www.tines.com/stories/agents/) includes agents and governed interactions, while its [deployment guidance](https://www.tines.com/blog/understanding-your-tines-deployment-options/) covers cloud and self-hosted options.

Buyers should still evaluate the workflow portfolio, licensing requirements, deployment responsibility, model policy, governance needs, and billing unit. A feature checklist alone will not show how a platform behaves under a representative workload.

## How much does Tines cost?

As of November 2026, [Tines pricing](https://www.tines.com/pricing/) lists a free edition and paid editions. The vendor asks buyers to discuss full paid pricing, packaging, and deployment requirements with its team.

The comparison should extend beyond subscription terms. Model charges, execution limits, user access, support, deployment operations, and governance requirements can all affect total cost. Competing products meter different units, including workflow executions, tasks, credits, platform usage, and model consumption.

A representative test should include one frequent deterministic workflow, one AI-intensive workflow, one human review flow, and one peak-volume scenario. This is more informative than comparing entry prices alone.

## Which Tines alternative should your team choose?

Choose based on the operating model rather than a universal ranking:

- Choose **Sim** when AI agents, model flexibility, customer-managed deployment, and an Apache 2.0 core matter.
- Choose [**n8n**](https://docs.n8n.io/deploy/host-n8n/deploy-with-the-ai-starter-kit) when a technical team wants broad automation and self-hosting.
- Choose [**Workato**](https://www.workato.com/agentstudio) when centralized enterprise integration and governance drive the decision.
- Choose [**Zapier**](https://zapier.com/) when hosted app automation and ease of adoption matter.
- Choose [**Make**](https://www.make.com/en/product) when visual scenario design and credit-based hosted automation fit the workload.
- Choose [**Gumloop**](https://docs.gumloop.com/core-concepts/workbooks) when hosted document and data workflows dominate the use case.

A proof of concept should use the same workflow, data, model, review step, and expected output on every shortlisted platform. The test exposes differences in build time, reliability, governance, infrastructure control, and cost before the team commits.
