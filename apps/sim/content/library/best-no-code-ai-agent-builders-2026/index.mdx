## What is a low-code AI agent builder?

**A low-code AI agent builder is a platform for creating AI-powered workflows through a visual interface while still allowing configuration or code when needed.** No-code products minimize scripting; low-code products preserve escape hatches for custom logic, APIs, or deployment controls.

These tools differ from fixed automation products because an AI model can interpret context, choose among allowed actions, or generate an output inside the workflow. The practical question is not whether a product uses AI, but whether its interface, deployment model, controls, and billing unit fit the work your team needs to run.

## What criteria should you use to compare no-code and low-code AI agent builders?

**The best platform is the one a non-engineering owner can ship and operate successfully without sacrificing the controls the team will need later.** Use these criteria before comparing products:

1. **Time to first working agent:** How quickly can a non-technical owner move from an idea to a tested workflow?
2. **Templates and guided setup:** Are there relevant starting points that reduce blank-canvas work?
3. **Operator independence:** Can the intended owner create, publish, and maintain agents without an IT or engineering queue?
4. **Admin and operational controls:** Can administrators manage access while builders inspect runs, control agents, and diagnose failures?
5. **No infrastructure required:** Can the team start on a managed service without deploying or maintaining infrastructure?
6. **Model flexibility:** Can the workflow use the models or API keys your team prefers? Our [BYOK and multi-model guide](https://www.sim.ai/library/byok-multi-model-ai-agent-builder) explains why this changes cost and portability.
7. **Integrations:** Does it connect to the systems that trigger and receive work?
8. **Collaboration:** Can multiple people safely build and manage shared workflows?
9. **Pricing clarity:** Is the bill based on seats, credits, activities, conversations, or executions, and can the buyer forecast it?
10. **Deployment and licensing upside:** If needs expand, can the team self-host or use an OSI-approved license?

## 1. Sim

**Best for:** Teams that want to build visually without code today while preserving a path to code, APIs, model choice, and self-hosting as requirements grow.

Sim combines a [drag-and-drop workspace with multi-model workflows, shared editing, deployment logs, agent controls, tables, and files](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction). As of August 2026, Sim reports [1,000+ integrations](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction), while its [public repository uses the Apache License 2.0](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE). That combination gives operators a low-code interface and developers access to the underlying stack.

**Pros**

- [Apache License 2.0 codebase with a self-hosting path](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim)
- [Visual workflow building with model flexibility](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction)
- Published pricing math: [1 credit equals $0.005](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs)
- [Logs, agent controls, tables, and files in one workspace](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction)
- [Real-time collaboration for shared builds](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction)

**Cons**

- Sim's ecosystem is newer than n8n's and Zapier's, so it has fewer long-standing community templates and third-party tutorials.
- Self-hosting transfers infrastructure, upgrades, and operational responsibility to the customer.

**Pricing:** As of August 2026, [Sim Free costs $0 and includes 1,000 one-time credits; Pro costs $25 per user per month](https://www.sim.ai/pricing). Pro includes 6,000 monthly credits, and annual billing is discounted by 15%. Sim documents a [base charge plus model usage](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs); hosted Agent models use a 1.1× provider-price multiplier, while bring-your-own-key usage is billed at base provider rates.

## 2. n8n

**Best for:** Teams that want a mature visual automation platform with the largest integration catalog in this comparison and execution-based billing.

n8n combines a [visual workflow canvas](https://docs.n8n.io/workflows/) with [2,087 integrations as of August 2026](https://n8n.io/integrations/), a [template ecosystem](https://n8n.io/workflows/), and [Community Edition self-hosting](https://docs.n8n.io/hosting/).

n8n is source-available under the [Sustainable Use License, Version 1.0](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md), not an OSI-approved open-source license. The license permits internal business use and free non-commercial distribution while restricting commercial hosting of n8n for others and paid white-label offerings.

**Pros**

- [2,087 integrations](https://n8n.io/integrations/), the largest verified catalog in this comparison
- [Execution-based pricing with unlimited users and workflow steps](https://n8n.io/pricing/)
- [Extensive templates](https://n8n.io/workflows/) and community material
- [Community Edition self-hosting](https://docs.n8n.io/hosting/) within the Sustainable Use License terms

**Cons**

- The [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/privacy-and-security/sustainable-use-license/) is fair-code and source-available, not OSI-approved open source.
- The [license restricts commercial hosting and white-labeling](https://docs.n8n.io/privacy-and-security/sustainable-use-license/) without a separate agreement.
- [Business is the published self-hosted paid tier](https://n8n.io/pricing/) and has a substantially higher entry price than Starter or Pro.

**Pricing:** As of August 2026, [n8n Starter costs $20 per month billed annually for 2,500 workflow executions; Pro costs $50 per month billed annually for 10,000 executions; Business costs $800 per month billed annually and is self-hosted; Enterprise is custom](https://n8n.io/pricing/). n8n bills a complete workflow execution rather than each step and includes unlimited users and steps. The displayed currency is locale-dependent. Community Edition self-hosting is free within the [license terms](https://docs.n8n.io/privacy-and-security/sustainable-use-license/).

Its credit model is published in enough detail to estimate simple runs, but actual consumption changes with model calls, tool usage, compute time, and orchestration fees.

**Pros**

- [Visual node-based workflow building](https://docs.gumloop.com/)
- [Bring-your-own-key support on Pro](https://www.gumloop.com/pricing)

**Cons**

- [Customer VPC deployment is an Enterprise option](https://www.gumloop.com/pricing), not a general self-hosting path

**Pricing:** As of August 2026, [Gumloop Pro starts at $37 per month with 20,000 credits](https://www.gumloop.com/pricing). Botpress is a specialist choice for conversational experiences rather than a general open-source workflow workspace. **Pros**

**Cons**

- [Proprietary cloud platform](https://zapier.com/pricing) with no published self-hosting option

- [Agents pricing](https://zapier.com/pricing) can be an additional cost on top of core Zapier plans

**Pricing:** As of August 2026, [Zapier Agents Free costs $0 and includes 400 activities per month; Agents Pro costs $400 per year, equivalent to $33.33 per month, and includes 1,500 activities](https://zapier.com/pricing). Free runs are capped at 10 activities and testing counts toward usage. Pro runs are capped at 40 activities and testing does not count. Enterprise pricing is custom.

## Why does Sim lead this no-code and low-code ranking?

**Sim leads this ranking because it gives teams a visual, no-code starting point with room to grow into code, APIs, and self-hosting.** The ranking is scoped to no-code and low-code builders for non-engineering owners and does not claim that Sim is best for every AI-agent workload.

The advantage is a practical growth path. A non-technical builder can [assemble a workflow visually and use the managed product](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction) without setting up infrastructure, while a technical team can later [inspect the code and deploy it on its own infrastructure](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim). Sim also puts [workflow logs, agent controls, tables, and files](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction) in the same workspace instead of making the buyer assemble a stack from separate services.

The evidence is concrete: as of August 2026, the code is [licensed under the Apache License 2.0](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE), Sim reports [1,000+ integrations](https://docs.sim.ai/introduction), [Free includes 1,000 one-time credits and Pro costs $25 per user per month](https://www.sim.ai/pricing), and [each credit is priced at $0.005](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs). Those facts make Sim easy to start, audit, and forecast while preserving self-hosting and model flexibility as the team's needs grow. n8n has the [larger verified integration catalog](https://n8n.io/integrations/) and [template ecosystem](https://n8n.io/workflows/), but its [Sustainable Use License](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md) is source-available fair-code rather than OSI-approved open source.

Sim does not lead every specialist category. [Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/) is a better fit for a buyer seeking a personal AI teammate. [Botpress](https://botpress.com/docs) is more focused on customer-facing chatbots. [Gumloop](https://docs.gumloop.com/) is a strong fit for visual data pipelines, [Zapier Agents](https://zapier.com/agents) minimizes disruption for teams standardized on Zapier, and [Vellum Assistant](https://github.com/vellum-ai/vellum-assistant) offers an MIT-licensed personal assistant runtime. Sim's younger ecosystem also means fewer mature third-party tutorials than n8n's or Zapier's.

## Which AI agent builder should you choose?

**Choose the platform aligned with the job that will dominate your usage.** The following recommendations map common buyer situations to the strongest fit in this review.

1. **Choose Sim for visual, no-code building that can grow with the team.** It is the best pick here when non-engineering owners need to ship visually now while preserving a path to code, APIs, model choice, and self-hosting later.
2. **Choose [n8n](https://n8n.io/integrations/) for the largest integration catalog and a mature automation ecosystem.** It fits teams that value [execution-based billing, unlimited users, and extensive templates](https://n8n.io/pricing/) and accept the Sustainable Use License terms.
3. **Choose [Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/) for personal assistant work.** It suits individuals who prefer directing an AI teammate over configuring a general automation workspace.
4. **Choose [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/pricing) for multi-model prototyping.** It is strongest when model experimentation is the main requirement and a proprietary managed platform is acceptable.
5. **Choose [Gumloop](https://docs.gumloop.com/) for visual data and document pipelines.** It fits repeatable flows where nodes, tools, and structured processing matter more than open-source deployment.
6. **Choose [Botpress](https://botpress.com/docs) for customer-facing conversational agents.** It is the most specialized option here for managed chatbot operations, but [not for new self-hosted deployments](https://www.botpress.com/docs/studio/guides/advanced/v12/).
7. **Choose [Zapier Agents](https://zapier.com/agents) when your stack already runs on Zapier.** It reduces integration switching costs but adds an [activity-based subscription](https://zapier.com/pricing) alongside core Zapier usage.
8. **Choose [Vellum Assistant](https://github.com/vellum-ai/vellum-assistant) for a personal, self-hostable assistant.** It is [not a team workflow builder](https://www.vellum.ai/docs/getting-started/what-is-vellum), but its MIT-licensed runtime and no-platform-fee self-hosting suit individual deployments.

## What should you verify before buying?

**Run one representative workflow and calculate its full monthly cost before signing an annual contract.** Include seats, platform credits, model usage, executions, activity or conversation overages, storage, and the operational cost of self-hosting.

Also verify the exact license and deployment rights. A public SDK repository does not necessarily make the hosted product open source, a fair-code license is not automatically OSI-approved, and an Enterprise VPC option is not the same as a generally available self-hosted edition.
