## TL;DR

Glean fits enterprises that need company-wide, permissions-aware search and discovery across many business systems. Dust fits teams that want to configure AI agents around selected company knowledge, models, instructions, and tools.

Both products extend beyond simple knowledge chat, so the decision is not strictly search versus agents. The practical difference is where each platform starts: Glean starts with enterprise-wide knowledge discovery and governance, while Dust starts with configurable agents for specific teams and use cases.

## Glean vs Dust for enterprise AI: which should we choose?

Choose Glean when the primary requirement is to help employees find information across many systems without bypassing source permissions. Its fit is a broad rollout in which search relevance, identity, permissions, and centralized administration matter more than deeply customized workflow logic.

Choose Dust when the primary requirement is to let teams configure agents with specific instructions, models, data sources, and tools. Its fit is a focused rollout for teams that want greater control over agent behavior than a conventional enterprise search interface provides.

Neither choice should be made from an agent demo alone. CIOs and Heads of AI should test permission fidelity, connector depth, answer citations, administrative controls, tool execution, auditability, deployment requirements, and total cost for the expected user population.

## How do Glean and Dust compare for enterprise AI?

Glean centers enterprise-wide search and permission-aware discovery. Dust centers flexible team-built agents grounded in selected company data.

## What are the key facts about Glean and Dust?

Glean and Dust differ most clearly in product center of gravity, deployment model, licensing, and billing.

- Glean combines enterprise search with assistance and agents, so its knowledge index and permission model are central to the product. Glean documents both [vendor-hosted and customer-hosted deployment models](https://docs.glean.com/get-started/prepare/about-deployment).
- Dust publishes its repository under the [MIT License](https://github.com/dust-tt/dust/blob/main/LICENSE) and sells hosted access through self-serve and custom Enterprise options as of November 2026.
- Glean's [connectors](https://docs.glean.com/connectors/connectors-power-glean) feed a unified, permission-aware knowledge layer used by Search, Chat, Assistant, and Agents.
- Dust combines connected company data with configurable agents, so [agent instructions, model selection, data scopes, and tools](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/developer-platform/overview/developer-platform) are central to the product.

A public repository does not automatically make customer-operated deployment simple or provide every capability of a vendor-hosted enterprise plan. Enterprises evaluating Dust should compare the exact repository features, deployment burden, support model, and hosted controls they require.

## When should an enterprise choose Glean?

Glean fits an enterprise that needs one permissions-aware discovery layer spanning many departments and systems.

Glean's core proposition is that employees can search company information and receive answers without manually checking each connected application. The platform's [enterprise search product](https://www.glean.com/enterprise-search) is relevant when the buying committee prioritizes broad connector coverage, relevance, identity mapping, source permissions, and adoption across a large workforce.

Glean also offers [Glean Agents](https://docs.glean.com/agents/overview/about-agents), so buyers should not treat it as search-only. Its agents can build on an enterprise knowledge foundation designed for organization-wide discovery, use tools, take actions, and run from supported schedules or events.

Glean may be a weaker fit when the main requirement is to model a long operational process with specialized branches, approvals, retries, transformations, and writes across systems. Buyers with that requirement should benchmark workflow-oriented platforms separately rather than assuming an enterprise search purchase will replace one.

## When should an enterprise choose Dust?

Dust fits teams that need configurable agents for specific knowledge-intensive jobs and want control over each agent's instructions, models, data, and tools.

Dust organizes connected information and agent access through [data sources](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/user-documentation/data-sources/overview) and [Spaces](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/user-documentation/admins/spaces-management). That structure can suit teams that want a research assistant, support agent, internal expert, or another role-specific agent without beginning with a company-wide search rollout.

Dust's public [developer documentation](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/developer-platform/overview/developer-platform) and [repository](https://github.com/dust-tt/dust) make its technical approach inspectable. The MIT-licensed repository is relevant to engineering-led evaluations, although buyers should separately assess the work required to operate the available code and the differences between it and Dust's hosted commercial service.

Dust now documents [schedule and webhook triggers](https://docs.dust.tt/docs/user-documentation/agents/triggers), so it should not be treated as chat-only. Teams should still test whether Dust provides the branching, approvals, state handling, retries, observability, and operational integrations required for each process.

## We use Dust for company knowledge but need workflow automation too. What should we evaluate?

Sim fits teams that need governed, multi-step agent workflows that move beyond knowledge chat into triggers, branching, API calls, human input, and actions across systems. Sim is the open-source AI workspace where teams build, deploy, and manage AI agents.

The required evaluation is different from a Glean-versus-Dust search evaluation. The team should test whether a platform can start work from schedules, webhooks, application events, or APIs; maintain state across multiple steps; route failures; pause for human input; restrict credentials; record execution history; and deploy workflows as controlled operational services. The [AI workflow automation platform buyer's checklist](https://www.sim.ai/library/ai-workflow-automation-platform-buyers-checklist) provides a fuller set of evaluation criteria.

Several platform categories can fit:

|---|---|---|
| Sim | Sim provides a visual builder for multi-step agent workflows and fits teams that need controlled deployment and execution visibility | Sim's core is Apache 2.0, while SSO, SCIM, access control, audit logs, data retention, and other code in `apps/sim/ee` use the separate [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE); production use of that enterprise code requires an active Sim Enterprise subscription |
| n8n | n8n fits node-based application automation that combines deterministic workflow steps with AI capabilities | As of November 2026, n8n uses the [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license/), a fair-code license with use restrictions |
| Microsoft Copilot Studio and Power Automate | These products fit organizations standardized on Microsoft 365, Power Platform, and Microsoft identity and governance; Microsoft documents [connectors shared across Copilot Studio and Power Platform](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/advanced-connectors) | Buyers should test licensing, environment administration, connector entitlements, and the division of work between Copilot Studio and Power Automate using Microsoft's [licensing guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-studio/billing-licensing) |
| Glean or Dust | Glean and Dust remain appropriate when the process is primarily knowledge retrieval, analysis, assistance, and a defined set of tool actions | Buyers should prove that either platform supports the required triggers, branches, approvals, retries, writes, and execution monitoring before using it as a workflow system |

A team does not necessarily have to replace Dust. It can retain Dust for company-knowledge agents and use a workflow-oriented platform for processes that require durable orchestration, approvals, and cross-system actions. The deciding factor should be whether maintaining two governed platforms is simpler and safer than forcing one product to cover both jobs.

## Which proof-of-concept tests should enterprise buyers run?

Glean and Dust should be tested with the same permission-sensitive questions, source documents, users, and operational tasks before an enterprise makes a platform decision.

1. Select 20 to 30 representative questions with known correct answers and authoritative source documents.
2. Include users with different roles, departments, regions, and access rights.
3. Test whether answers cite the correct source and respect document-level permissions after access changes.
4. Measure connector completeness for the exact objects, comments, attachments, metadata, and access controls the organization uses.
5. Build one realistic agent in each platform rather than relying on vendor-provided demos.
6. Test an action that writes to another system, including approval, failure, retry, and audit requirements.
7. Review administrator controls for identity, provisioning, retention, logs, model access, and credential management.
8. Calculate expected cost using the actual number of users, builders, agents, model calls, and required enterprise features.
9. Ask security and legal teams to compare hosted data handling, subprocessors, residency options, contractual terms, and customer-hosted responsibilities.
10. Evaluate search quality and workflow capability separately so strong retrieval does not hide weak orchestration, or vice versa.
