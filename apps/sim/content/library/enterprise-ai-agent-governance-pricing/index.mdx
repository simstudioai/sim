## TL;DR

Enterprise AI agent platforms usually reserve company identity-provider sign-in (single sign-on, or SSO), automated user provisioning through SCIM, granular access limits based on roles (role-based access control, or RBAC), centralized audit logs, continuous delivery to security monitoring systems (SIEM streaming), contractual service-level agreements (SLAs), and dedicated support for enterprise plans sold through custom quotes. The real annual cost is driven by the platform subscription, users or usage, model spend, deployment model, support level, data controls, and implementation services, not by a universal per-feature surcharge.

As of November 2026, buyers should compare written quotes rather than assume that two Enterprise plans include equivalent governance capabilities. A lower platform fee can become more expensive if audit-log retention, SIEM export, identity lifecycle management, premium support, or model usage is billed separately.

## What should enterprise buyers know at a glance?

Enterprise AI agent platform pricing is easiest to compare when licensing, deployment, billing units, and governance entitlements are separated.

- [Sim](https://www.sim.ai/pricing) is the open-source AI workspace. [Sim's core](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE) is Apache 2.0, while SSO, SCIM, access control, audit logs, data drains, and other code in `apps/sim/ee` use the separate [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE), which requires an active Sim Enterprise subscription for production use. Sim can be [self-hosted](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/self-hosting), and model costs depend on the selected provider-key and deployment approach.
- [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/) is a self-hostable automation product whose public pricing is primarily based on monthly workflow executions, while its Enterprise plan uses custom pricing. Its [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license) is a source-available fair-code license rather than an OSI-approved open-source license.
- [Zapier](https://zapier.com/pricing) is a proprietary cloud product with task-oriented plan allowances. Its pricing page places audit logs, SSO through Security Assertion Markup Language (SAML), a standard for exchanging sign-in information with a company identity provider, SCIM, and other advanced administration controls in its plan comparison, with Enterprise handled through sales.

These public pricing models and enterprise-sales routes are current as of November 2026. The signed order form and security addendum determine what a specific customer receives.

## What do platforms charge for SSO, SCIM, RBAC, audit logs, SIEM streaming, SLAs, and support?

Enterprise AI agent platforms generally bundle advanced identity, audit, security operations, and support features into a custom enterprise contract instead of publishing a fixed price for each feature.

Procurement will usually receive one platform quote covering a negotiated package rather than a menu such as “SSO for $X” and “audit logs for $Y.” The quote may still contain separate line items for premium support, implementation, private deployment, longer log retention, extra environments, or usage commitments.

As of November 2026, [Sim](https://www.sim.ai/pricing), [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/), and [Zapier](https://zapier.com/pricing) direct buyers who need their broadest enterprise governance packages toward a sales conversation rather than publishing a universal dollar surcharge for the full SSO-and-audit package. Buyers need written answers about entitlements, limits, retention, and overages before comparing prices.

A useful comparison separates four cost categories:

1. **Platform access:** The base subscription, seats, workspaces, environments, or execution allowance.
2. **Governance:** SSO, SCIM, RBAC, audit logs, SIEM export, retention, data residency, and security-review access.
3. **Runtime consumption:** Workflow executions, tasks, compute, model tokens, storage, and network costs.
4. **Services and assurance:** Onboarding, implementation, SLA, dedicated support, training, and contractual compliance commitments.

## Which plan tiers usually include enterprise security and governance features?

Vendors commonly place identity lifecycle management, centralized audit export, data residency, and contractual service commitments above ordinary self-serve plans. The exact placement varies, so procurement should treat the following table as a request checklist rather than a promise about a particular vendor.

| Feature | Common sales placement | What procurement should confirm |
|---|---|---|
| Single sign-on (SSO) | Business or Enterprise package | Supported identity protocols, number of identity providers, domain enforcement, guest behavior, and break-glass access |
| Automated user provisioning (SCIM) | Enterprise package | User creation, deactivation, group synchronization, deprovisioning speed, and whether SCIM is separately priced |
| Role-based access control (RBAC) | Team, Business, or Enterprise package | Whether roles apply at organization, workspace, project, workflow, credential, and deployment levels |
| Audit logs | Business or Enterprise package | Events captured, actor identity, timestamps, retention period, export format, API access, and additional retention fees |
| SIEM log streaming | Enterprise or security add-on | Push versus pull delivery, supported destinations, delivery latency, retry behavior, filtering, and volume limits |
| Data residency | Enterprise or deployment add-on | Regions available and whether backups, logs, model traffic, and subprocessors remain in-region |
| SOC 2 report access | Security review or sales process | Report period, bridge letter availability, NDA requirements, and access conditions |
| SLA | Enterprise contract | Uptime definition, exclusions, support response targets, service credits, and claim procedure |
| Dedicated support | Enterprise or premium support add-on | Named contacts, coverage hours, escalation path, response targets, and whether technical account management costs extra |

“Available on Enterprise” is not a complete answer. A feature can be included but constrained by retention, event volume, geography, supported identity providers, or support hours.

For a deeper control checklist, read the [enterprise guide to AI agent identity, credentials, and audit trails](https://www.sim.ai/library/ai-agent-identity-credentials-audit-trails-enterprise-guide).

## How much do centralized audit logs and SIEM integrations cost?

Enterprise AI platforms usually price centralized audit logs and SIEM integrations inside a custom enterprise package, with cost affected by retention, event volume, delivery method, and support obligations.

Buyers should distinguish three capabilities that vendors sometimes group under “audit logging”:

- Searchable audit history in the product for administrators.
- Export through an API or downloadable files.
- Continuous streaming or draining into a SIEM such as Splunk or Microsoft Sentinel.

The third capability is normally the most operationally demanding because it requires reliable delivery, authentication, retries, monitoring, and a defined event schema. A vendor may include the connector while limiting retention, throughput, destinations, or support. For example, Zapier documents separate [audit log and log stream behavior](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/49374721349901-Understanding-the-audit-log-and-log-streams) for reviewing events in Zapier and sending events to an external endpoint.

Ask the vendor to quote the complete logging requirement: every event category, required retention, daily event volume, export latency, SIEM destination, support coverage, and evidence needed by auditors. This prevents a low initial quote from omitting the controls that security operations expects.

## How should a mid-size enterprise estimate the real annual cost?

Enterprise AI agent platform total cost should be calculated as the annual platform contract plus runtime consumption, model spend, deployment operations, implementation, support, and internal administration.

Use this model:

**Annual TCO = platform subscription + seat or usage charges + model spend + hosting and operations + implementation + support add-ons + compliance overhead**

Create one worksheet row for each component:

| Cost component | Input to request or estimate |
|---|---|
| Base platform | Annual subscription and included workspaces, environments, seats, tasks, or executions |
| Identity and access | SSO, SCIM, RBAC, access reviews, guest controls, and additional identity-provider fees |
| Audit and SIEM | Included retention, event limits, API or streaming access, destinations, and overages |
| Model usage | Provider token charges, hosted-key pricing, BYOK treatment, embeddings, and reranking |
| Cloud runtime | Tasks, executions, compute time, storage, network transfer, and concurrency |
| Self-hosting | Infrastructure, database, backups, upgrades, monitoring, security patches, and on-call labor |
| Support and SLA | Coverage hours, response targets, named contacts, service credits, and premium support fees |
| Rollout | Implementation, migration, training, security review, and integration engineering |
| Contract risk | Minimum commitment, annual uplift, overages, renewal terms, and exit or data-export costs |

Compare at least three scenarios: expected usage, twice expected usage, and a stress case with higher model consumption and log volume. The useful quote is the one that remains predictable under realistic growth, not merely the one with the lowest first-year base fee.

For a broader treatment of build and runtime expenses, see [How Much Does It Cost to Build and Run an AI Agent?](https://www.sim.ai/library/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-and-run-an-ai-agent).

## Does bring-your-own-key reduce enterprise AI agent platform costs?

Bring-your-own-key can reduce or clarify model costs, but BYOK does not remove platform, governance, hosting, or support charges.

Sim supports workspace BYOK keys on any Sim Cloud plan, while organization-level keys require Pro for Teams, Max for Teams, or Enterprise, as documented in [Sim's cost guide](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs). With BYOK, the customer pays the model provider directly for those calls, while other platform and execution charges can still apply.

BYOK also changes responsibility. The customer may need to manage provider contracts, quotas, regional processing, key rotation, abuse monitoring, and incident response. For a detailed explanation, see [How Sim's Bring-Your-Own-Key Works](https://www.sim.ai/library/byok-multi-model-ai-agent-builder).

## Is self-hosting cheaper than buying an enterprise cloud plan?

Self-hosting can lower vendor-controlled runtime charges, but it is cheaper only when infrastructure and operational labor cost less than the cloud premium it replaces.

A self-hosted estimate should include compute, database, storage, backups, observability, upgrades, vulnerability response, disaster recovery, identity integration, and on-call ownership. It should also distinguish open-source core functionality from separately licensed enterprise features.

[Sim's core](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE) is Apache 2.0, but Sim's SSO, SCIM, access control, audit logs, data drains, data retention, and related enterprise code are under the separate [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE). Development, testing, and internal non-production use of that enterprise code is free, while production use requires an active Sim Enterprise subscription.

n8n can also be self-hosted, but its [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license) is source-available rather than OSI-approved. Buyers should review the current license and enterprise terms instead of treating self-hosting as proof that every governance feature is free.

For a focused license comparison, see [Apache 2.0 vs Fair-Code](https://www.sim.ai/library/apache-2-0-vs-fair-code).

## What AI agent tools offer dedicated support and SLAs?

[Sim](https://www.sim.ai/pricing), [n8n](https://n8n.io/pricing/), and [Zapier](https://zapier.com/enterprise) offer enterprise sales paths for organizations that require negotiated support and service commitments. The signed contract determines the actual SLA and escalation coverage.

Procurement should not treat “priority support” as equivalent to a contractual SLA. A useful SLA states the covered service, uptime calculation, excluded events, severity definitions, initial response targets, restoration targets, escalation path, service credits, and claim window.

Dedicated support should also be defined precisely. Ask whether the quoted service includes a named technical account manager, an account executive, solution architecture, implementation help, 24/7 incident coverage, or only faster access to a shared queue.

## What should buyers ask vendors about enterprise governance pricing?

Enterprise AI agent vendors should answer the same written governance and pricing questionnaire so procurement can compare equivalent offers.

Ask these questions:

1. Which exact plan includes company identity-provider sign-in (SSO), automated user provisioning (SCIM), access limits based on roles (RBAC), continuous delivery to a security monitoring system (SIEM streaming), audit logs, data residency, a service-level agreement (SLA), and dedicated support?
2. Is any listed capability a separately priced add-on?
3. What user, workspace, environment, execution, task, storage, or event limits apply?
4. Which audit events are captured, and how long are they retained?
5. Can logs stream continuously to our SIEM, and are delivery volume or destinations limited?
6. Can SCIM deactivate users and revoke their active sessions and credentials promptly?
7. Which resources can RBAC protect, including workflows, credentials, deployments, logs, and administrative settings?
8. Does data residency cover backups, telemetry, logs, subprocessors, and model-provider traffic?
9. What does the SLA measure, and what remedies apply when the vendor misses it?
10. What support coverage, response targets, and escalation contacts are included?
11. How are model usage, tasks, executions, compute, storage, and overages billed?
12. Can we use our own model-provider keys, and does the platform add a markup?
13. What changes at renewal, including minimum commitments and annual price increases?
14. How can we export workflows, logs, credentials metadata, and other data when the contract ends?

The security review should test whether the product can enforce the promised controls, while the contract review should confirm that those controls remain available at the quoted price. [How to Pilot an AI Agent Platform Through Security Review](https://www.sim.ai/library/pilot-ai-agent-platform-security-review) explains how to validate those claims in a controlled rollout.

## How should CIOs compare platforms without choosing only on price?

CIOs should compare enterprise AI agent platforms on control coverage, total cost, deployment fit, support commitments, and operational evidence rather than the base quote alone.

Start with the controls the organization must enforce, then compare how each product implements identity, permissions, audit evidence, model access, deployment, and support. Sim fits teams that need to build, deploy, and manage AI agents in one workspace with enterprise controls available under a separately licensed enterprise offering.

The lowest defensible bid must satisfy the required controls in writing. If a vendor cannot state audit retention, SIEM delivery, deprovisioning behavior, support response targets, and overage rules, procurement does not yet have a comparable price.
