## Why do teams look for a Zapier alternative?

**Teams leave Zapier when its task-based pricing, cloud-only deployment, or linear trigger-and-action model stops fitting the work.** Zapier remains a practical choice for connecting SaaS apps quickly, and a fair comparison starts with how it is priced.

Zapier counts [a task as any successful action that runs in Zapier](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496196837261-How-is-task-usage-measured-in-Zapier). Triggers, Filter and Paths steps, failed actions, and built-in apps such as Formatter, Delay, and Looping do not count. In Zapier's own example, a Zap with two actions that runs ten times uses 20 tasks, so a five-action Zap that runs 1,000 times a month uses 5,000 tasks.

As of October 2026, [Zapier's pricing page](https://zapier.com/pricing) lists:

- **Free:** 100 tasks a month, with two-step Zaps only.
- **Professional:** from $19.99 a month billed annually ($29.99 billed monthly) for 750 tasks, with multi-step Zaps, premium apps, and webhooks.
- **Team:** from $69 a month billed annually ($103.50 billed monthly) for 2,000 tasks, with 25 users, shared Zaps, and SAML SSO.
- **Enterprise:** contact sales.

The price rises with the task tier you select, from 750 tasks up to 2 million. Zapier's plan data lists these Professional tiers:

| Monthly tasks | Billed annually (per month) | Billed monthly |
|---|---|---|
| 750 | $19.99 | $29.99 |
| 2,000 | $49 | $73.50 |
| 10,000 | $129 | $193.50 |
| 100,000 | $489 | $733.50 |

That five-action Zap running 1,000 times a month needs the 5,000-task tier, which costs $89 per month billed annually ($133.50 billed monthly).

Zapier also says that "AI steps, code, and SDK now all follow the same task-based pricing model." Its task documentation lists the rates: Code by Zapier uses one task per run at standard runtime, AI by Zapier depends on the model tier, and each successful call through Zapier MCP counts as two tasks. MCP (Model Context Protocol) is a standard way for AI assistants and agents to connect to outside apps and tools. [Pay-per-task billing](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/15279018245901-How-pay-per-task-billing-works-in-Zapier) is on by default for accounts that joined after January 2024, so Zaps keep running past the plan limit, up to three times the plan's task allowance.

Zapier Agents is priced separately for now. Agents use [an activity quota instead of tasks](https://zapier.com/pricing): the free tier runs up to 400 activities a month, and Agents Pro costs $50 billed monthly or $400 billed annually ($33.33 a month) for 1,500 activities. Zapier is [migrating Agents to AI by Zapier](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/47402591569805-Migrating-from-Agents-to-AI-by-Zapier), which "uses the same task-based pricing as the rest of Zapier," and it has not set a date to turn off Agents.

Pipedream is no longer ranked here. [Pipedream Workflows is in maintenance mode](https://pipedream.com/docs/workflows) and no longer accepts signups or subscriptions. Existing workflows keep running through March 31, 2027, and all Workflows data, including connected-account credentials, is deleted by April 30, 2027. Pipedream's export tool downloads the structure of your workflows as JSON, one project at a time, so steps, credentials, and triggers still need rebuilding elsewhere. Pipedream Connect continues. Teams leaving it can read [Sim vs Pipedream](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/pipedream) or compare [Pipedream alternatives with visual builders](https://www.sim.ai/library/best-pipedream-alternatives-2026).

## What are the 15 best Zapier alternatives in 2026?

**Each tool below has a clear best fit, a sourced price, and at least one real limitation.**

### 1. Sim

**Best for:** Teams that want to build, deploy, and manage AI agents visually, conversationally in Chat, or with code, with the option to self-host. For a head-to-head view, see [Sim vs Zapier](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/zapier) and [Sim as an open-source Zapier alternative](https://www.sim.ai/library/sim-open-source-zapier-alternative).

**Pricing:** The [Free plan](https://www.sim.ai/pricing) includes 1,000 one-time credits (worth $5). Pro is $25 per user per month with 6,000 monthly credits, Max is $100 per user per month with 25,000 monthly credits, and Enterprise is custom. The Apache 2.0 core can be self-hosted without license fees.

**Strengths:** Agents, models, and integrations live in one workspace with Chat, Tables, Files, a Knowledge Base, and Logs that trace every run block by block. Sim connects to 28 model providers, and [workspace BYOK keys](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/costs) work on every plan, so you can pay model providers directly. A [self-hosted deployment](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/self-hosting) can use local models through Ollama, vLLM, LM Studio, or LiteLLM without Enterprise.

**Limitations:** Sim's 270+ integrations are far fewer than Zapier's 9,000+, so check your long-tail apps first. Free credits are one-time, not monthly. Self-hosting means your team runs upgrades, backups, and monitoring, and enterprise features such as SSO, SCIM, and audit logs use the [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE), which requires an Enterprise subscription for production use.

**Sources:** [Sim pricing](https://www.sim.ai/pricing), [Sim core license](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE), [self-hosting docs](https://docs.sim.ai/platform/self-hosting).

### 2. Make

**Best for:** Operations teams that want to see branching, routing, and data transformations laid out visually. See [Sim vs Make](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/make).

**Pricing:** [Make's pricing page](https://www.make.com/en/pricing) lists Free with 1,000 credits a month, and Core from $9, Pro from $16, and Teams from $29 a month with annual billing. Non-AI apps use [one credit per operation](https://help.make.com/credits), while credits for Make's own AI Provider are "based on tokens and operations."

**Strengths:** Routers and filters are included from the free plan, and Make lists 3,000+ apps. Make AI Agents, currently in beta, are included on all plans and use Make's AI Provider, or your own model key on paid plans.

**Limitations:** Make is cloud-only. Its [on-premise agent](https://help.make.com/on-premise-agent), which reaches apps on your local network, "is available to Enterprise customers." The free plan has a 15-minute minimum interval between runs, and AI-heavy scenarios are harder to estimate. Sim is the closer fit when agents, not app syncing, are the main job, or when you need to self-host.

**Sources:** [Make pricing](https://www.make.com/en/pricing), [Make credits](https://help.make.com/credits), [Make on-premise agent](https://help.make.com/on-premise-agent).

### 3. n8n

**Best for:** Technical teams that want to self-host, add JavaScript or Python steps, and pay per workflow run. See [Sim vs n8n](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/n8n) and the wider list of [n8n alternatives](https://www.sim.ai/library/n8n-alternatives).

**Pricing:** [n8n's pricing page](https://n8n.io/pricing/) says pricing "is based on monthly workflow executions, regardless of complexity." Starter is €20 a month billed annually for 2,500 executions, Pro is €50 for 10,000, Business is €667 for 40,000 and is self-hosted only, and Enterprise is custom. The Community Edition is a free self-hosted version on GitHub.

**Strengths:** One execution covers a whole run with unlimited steps, which favors long workflows. All plans include unlimited users and workflows, and the AI Agent node, MCP Server Trigger, and MCP Client are on every cloud plan.

**Limitations:** n8n is not open source. Its [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license) is a fair-code license that allows use "only for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use," so offering n8n to your customers needs a separate agreement. Sim fits teams that want an OSI-approved Apache 2.0 core and agents built conversationally as well as visually.

**Sources:** [n8n pricing](https://n8n.io/pricing/), [n8n license docs](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license), [n8n LICENSE file](https://github.com/n8n-io/n8n/blob/master/LICENSE.md).

### 4. Activepieces

**Best for:** Small teams that want simple, low-cost automation with the option to run it on their own servers.

**Pricing:** [Activepieces pricing](https://www.activepieces.com/pricing) lists Free at $0 with 1,000 credits a month and no card, Plus at "$20 flat per month" for 10,000 credits, Team at $200 a month for 50,000 credits and 25 users, and custom Ultimate. A run is one credit however many steps it has. AI steps cost 2, 10, or 20 credits by model tier, or 1 credit with your own keys on Plus and above.

**Strengths:** The [core is MIT-licensed](https://www.activepieces.com/docs/about/license), and every plan lists cloud or on-prem hosting. Run-based pricing keeps multi-step automations predictable, and Activepieces lists 760+ integrations.

**Limitations:** Enterprise and cloud features use a commercial license, and the pricing page says its "SOC 2 program is in progress." It is closer to traditional Zapier flows than to a full agent workspace. Sim is the closer fit when agents need models, tools, and a Knowledge Base in one place.

**Sources:** [Activepieces pricing](https://www.activepieces.com/pricing), [Activepieces license](https://www.activepieces.com/docs/about/license).

### 5. Microsoft Power Automate

**Best for:** Organizations whose identity, data, and apps already center on Microsoft 365, Dynamics 365, and Teams. See [Sim vs Power Automate](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/power-automate).

**Pricing:** [Microsoft's pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/products/power-automate/pricing) lists a free 30-day trial, Premium at $15.00 per user per month paid yearly, Process at $150.00 per bot per month paid yearly, and Hosted Process at $215.00 per bot per month paid yearly.

**Strengths:** Premium covers cloud flows, attended desktop automation (RPA), and process mining. Microsoft offers both [user and capacity licensing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/power-automate-licensing/types), so unattended bots can be licensed separately from people.

**Limitations:** Licensing is harder to compare than a single plan price, and building custom agents is sold separately through Microsoft Copilot Studio. Power Automate is not self-hostable. Sim fits teams that want agents across non-Microsoft tools and models from many providers.

**Sources:** [Power Automate pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/products/power-automate/pricing), [Power Automate license types](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/power-platform/admin/power-automate-licensing/types).

### 6. Workato

**Best for:** Large organizations that run an IT-governed integration program across departments. See [Sim vs Workato](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/workato).

**Pricing:** Workato's main [pricing page](https://www.workato.com/pricing) lists no prices and asks you to schedule a demo. Its [self-service plan docs](https://docs.workato.com/en/pricing/self-service/self-service.html) list Free with 50K one-time credits and Pro from $75 a month for 2,500 credits, rising through higher credit tiers to $1,275 a month for 50,000 credits. The [self-serve signup page](https://www.workato.com/free) shows one of those tiers, $100 a month for 3.5k credits, with up to 3 users per workspace. Enterprise is custom pricing. Its [billing docs](https://docs.workato.com/en/pricing) describe usage-based billing, with a separate model for customers who joined before February 2024.

**Strengths:** Enterprise adds unlimited users and workspaces, lifecycle and environment management, and multi-region deployment. An [on-prem agent](https://docs.workato.com/on-prem.html) creates a secure connection from your private network to Workato cloud.

**Limitations:** Enterprise pricing is not public, and the on-prem agent connects to Workato cloud rather than hosting the platform. Sim fits teams that want to self-host their agent infrastructure and start without a sales cycle.

## Which Zapier alternatives are free, open source, or self-hosted?

**Make, Activepieces, IFTTT, Integrately, Albato, and Pabbly Connect have ongoing free plans, Bardeen includes 100 free credits, and Sim, n8n, and Activepieces can run on your own servers.** Those are separate questions: a free hosted plan caps usage, and a self-hosted deployment removes license fees but not infrastructure costs.

- **Sim:** core under [Apache 2.0](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE), an OSI-approved open-source license. Enterprise features use the [Sim Enterprise License](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/apps/sim/ee/LICENSE).
- **Activepieces:** core under MIT, with cloud and enterprise features under a [commercial license](https://www.activepieces.com/docs/about/license).
- **n8n:** source-available under the [Sustainable Use License](https://docs.n8n.io/n8n-community-license), which is not OSI-approved.
- **Zapier, Make, Power Automate, Workato, Tray.ai, and the rest:** vendor-hosted. Make and Workato offer on-prem agents that reach private systems, which is not the same as hosting the platform.

Self-hosting moves upgrades, security patches, monitoring, backups, and model costs to your team. For a licensing-focused shortlist, see [open-source AI agent platforms](https://www.sim.ai/library/open-source-ai-agent-platforms) and [Apache 2.0 vs fair-code](https://www.sim.ai/library/apache-2-0-vs-fair-code).

## How should you choose a Zapier alternative?

**Start from the work you need to run, the people who will maintain it, and the billing unit that grows with your usage.**

- **Choose Sim if** AI agents are central and you want model choice, Chat, code, and a self-hostable Apache 2.0 core.
- **Choose Make if** operations teams want visual routing and a low-cost managed service.
- **Choose n8n if** developers want to self-host and pay per workflow run.
- **Choose Activepieces if** you want run-based pricing and an MIT core.
- **Choose Power Automate if** you are standardized on Microsoft 365.
- **Choose Workato or Tray.ai if** IT governs integrations across many departments.
- **Choose Gumloop, Lindy, Relevance AI, or Bardeen if** you want a hosted agent product for a specific team.
- **Choose IFTTT, Integrately, Albato, or Pabbly Connect if** you need simple, cheap app automations.

The billing units are not interchangeable. For 1,000 monthly runs of a five-action workflow, Zapier counts 5,000 tasks, n8n counts 1,000 executions (within the 2,500 on Starter), Activepieces counts 1,000 credits if no step uses AI (its free monthly allowance), and Make uses about one credit per module per run. Model costs come on top everywhere, so rebuild one real workflow in your top two picks before moving the rest.

Also decide whether the work needs a fixed automation or an AI agent:

| | Simple automation (Zapier-style) | AI agent (Sim) |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Structured fields from a known app | Emails, documents, chats, or web pages |
| Path | Fixed steps decided in advance | The agent chooses tools and next steps |
| Example | Add every new form lead to the CRM | Read each lead's email, research the company, score it, and draft a reply |
| Failure mode | Breaks when a field changes | Needs guardrails, review, and run logs |
| Cost driver | Steps or tasks per run | Model and tool usage per run |

Many teams end up with both kinds of work: deterministic steps for syncing data and an agent only where judgment is needed. [AI-native vs traditional workflow automation](https://www.sim.ai/library/ai-native-workflow-automation-vs-traditional-automation) explains that split, the [AI workflow automation buyer's checklist](https://www.sim.ai/library/ai-workflow-automation-platform-buyers-checklist) lists what to test, and the [best AI agent platforms in 2026](https://www.sim.ai/library/best-ai-agent-platforms-2026) covers the wider agent category.
