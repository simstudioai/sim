The cheapest way to run an AI agent in 2026 is to self-host an open-source builder such as Sim, n8n's Community Edition, or Dify's Community Edition and pay only for a server and model usage. If you would rather not run infrastructure, the lowest hosted entry prices on annual billing are Make Core at $9 a month, Zapier Professional from $19.99 a month, n8n Starter at €20 a month, and Sim Pro at $21 per user per month ($25 billed monthly), and several builders have a free plan or trial to test on first.

This article is published by Sim, and Sim is one of the eight products covered. Prices were checked against each vendor's pricing page on November 26, 2026. Prices change, so confirm them before you commit.

> **Choose self-hosting when** you have someone who can run a server and you want the lowest cash cost.
>
> **Choose a hosted free plan when** you want to test an agent on real work this week without a card.
>
> **Choose Sim when** you want to build visually, in Chat, or with code on an Apache 2.0 core, use your own model keys without a markup, and add teammates on the same agents when you are ready.

## What is the cheapest AI agent platform with solid features?

There are two kinds of cheap, and they cost different things.

- **Self-hosted, no license fee.** Sim, n8n, and Dify publish editions you can run yourself. The software costs nothing, but a server, model usage, updates, and security are on you.
- **Hosted, low entry price.** Make, Zapier, n8n, and Sim have paid plans from $9 to $21 a month for one builder on annual billing. You trade a monthly fee for not running anything.

For a startup, the deciding cost is usually not the plan price but the unit the plan meters. An agent that runs ten steps and three model calls per run burns through a per-task or per-credit allowance much faster than one that runs a single step.

## Which builder has the best free tier for testing before you buy?

The best free tier for testing is one that lets you run the exact agent you plan to buy, with your real integrations, for long enough to see a week of results.

- **Recurring hosted allowances** suit agents that run on a schedule. Make offers up to 1,000 credits a month, and Zapier 100 tasks a month with two-step Zaps.
- **One-time allowances and trials** suit a focused evaluation. Sim's Free plan gives 1,000 one-time credits with all 280+ integrations and no card, and new Sim Pro subscribers get a 14-day trial with 2,000 credits and Sim-hosted models, with a card required. Zapier offers a 14-day Professional trial and n8n Cloud a trial, both with no card. Gumloop offers 14 days of Pro. Lindy's $50 in credits last 7 days.
- **Self-hosted editions** have no time limit at all, so they suit long evaluations if you can run a server.

On Sim's Free plan, models and tools run on your own provider keys, and each run carries a base charge of 1 credit. If every model call in an agent uses your keys, the 1,000 credits cover up to 1,000 runs, with sandboxed code and hosted tools costing extra.

## What does each platform cost a small startup team?

Sim, the publisher, is listed first; the others follow in order of entry price, with the trade-off for each stated plainly.

### 1. Sim

**Best for:** Small teams that want to build visually, in Chat, or with code, bring their own model keys, and keep the option to self-host.

**What it costs:** The Free plan is $0 with 1,000 one-time credits, one workspace, two concurrent executions, and all 280+ integrations on your own keys. Pro is $25 per user per month with 6,000 credits a month plus a weekly refresh of 2,000 more, three workspaces, teammate invites, and Sim-hosted models. New Pro subscribers get a 14-day free trial with 2,000 credits, including Sim-hosted models and tools; a card is required at checkout, and Pro is charged when the trial ends unless you cancel first. Max is $100 per user per month with 25,000 credits a month plus a weekly refresh of 4,000. Annual billing costs 15% less. One credit is $0.005, and model calls on your own keys add no Sim usage charge beyond the 1-credit base charge per run.

**Limitations:** The Free plan's credits do not renew and it does not include teammate invites, so a team that runs agents every day will need Pro or a self-hosted deployment. Enterprise features such as SSO and access control need a license when self-hosted.

**Sources:** [Sim pricing](https://www.sim.ai/pricing), [Sim repository license](https://github.com/simstudioai/sim/blob/main/LICENSE).

### 2. Make

**Best for:** Teams that want a recurring hosted free allowance and detailed visual control.

**What it costs:** Make's Free plan offers up to 1,000 credits a month. On annual billing, Core starts at $9 a month for 10,000 credits a month and Pro at $16 a month, with prices rising as you buy more credits.

**Limitations:** Each module action consumes credits, so a multi-step agent uses the allowance faster than the headline number suggests.

**Sources:** [Make pricing](https://www.make.com/pricing), [Sim vs Make](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/make).

### 3. Zapier

**Best for:** Teams whose agents are short, simple automations between popular apps.

**What it costs:** The Free plan is $0 with 100 tasks a month and two-step Zaps. On annual billing, Professional starts at $19.99 a month and Team at $69 a month. New accounts get a 14-day Professional trial with no credit card.

**Limitations:** Zapier bills per task, so a multi-step agent that runs often moves up the task tiers quickly. For how that adds up, see [Zapier pricing at scale](https://www.sim.ai/library/zapier-pricing-at-scale).

**Sources:** [Zapier pricing](https://zapier.com/pricing), [Sim vs Zapier](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/zapier).

### 4. n8n

**Best for:** Technical teams that want per-execution billing or are happy to self-host.

**What it costs:** The self-hosted Community Edition has no license fee. The n8n Cloud trial needs no credit card. Starter is €20 a month billed annually for 2,500 workflow executions, and Pro is €50 a month billed annually for 10,000. n8n also lists a Startup plan for qualifying companies with fewer than 20 employees.

**Limitations:** n8n is fair-code under the Sustainable Use License rather than OSI-approved open source, and self-hosting means running updates and security yourself. For the full breakdown, see [n8n pricing for Cloud, self-hosted, and Enterprise](https://www.sim.ai/library/n8n-pricing-cloud-self-hosted-enterprise).

**Sources:** [n8n pricing](https://n8n.io/pricing/), [Sim vs n8n](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/n8n).

### 5. Gumloop

**Best for:** Teams that want a hosted builder and are ready to pay after a trial.

**What it costs:** Pro is $37 a month with 20,000 credits a month, after a 14-day free trial. Enterprise pricing is custom.

**Limitations:** There is no permanent free plan, so evaluation is limited to the trial window.

**Sources:** [Gumloop pricing](https://www.gumloop.com/pricing), [Sim vs Gumloop](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/gumloop).

### 6. Lindy

**Best for:** Teams that want a hosted AI assistant they can hand everyday tasks to.

**What it costs:** You start with $50 in free credits that last 7 days. Paid plans start at $49.99 a month for 5,000 credits per active user, and every active user in the workspace is billed.

**Limitations:** Per-user billing raises the cost as the team grows, and the free credits expire after a week.

**Sources:** [Lindy pricing](https://www.lindy.ai/pricing).

### 7. Dify

**Best for:** Developers building LLM apps who want a hosted sandbox or a self-deployed edition.

**What it costs:** The Sandbox plan is free with 200 message credits. Professional is $590 per workspace a year with 5,000 message credits a month, and Team is $1,590 per workspace a year. The Community Edition can be self-deployed under the Dify Open Source License, a modified Apache 2.0 license that requires a commercial license to run it as a multi-tenant service or to remove Dify's logo from its frontend.

**Limitations:** Paid plans are billed per workspace per year, and the Sandbox credits are sized for trying the product rather than running an agent daily.

**Sources:** [Dify pricing](https://dify.ai/pricing), [Sim vs Dify](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/dify).

### 8. Relevance AI

**Best for:** Sales and go-to-market teams buying an AI agent workforce through a sales process.

**What it costs:** Relevance AI offers a free start, but its pricing page publishes no self-serve plan prices; Enterprise is priced through sales.

**Limitations:** Without a listed price, a startup cannot compare it on cost until it talks to sales.

**Sources:** [Relevance AI pricing](https://relevanceai.com/pricing), [Sim vs Relevance AI](https://www.sim.ai/comparisons/relevance-ai).

## How do credits, tasks, and executions change the real price?

The same agent can cost very different amounts depending on what the plan counts.

- **Per execution (n8n).** One workflow run counts once, however many steps it has, so long agents are cheaper per run.
- **Per task (Zapier).** Each action step counts, so cost grows with the number of steps.
- **Per credit (Sim, Make, Gumloop, Lindy).** Credits are spent on runs, steps, or model calls depending on the vendor, so check what one credit buys.

Model usage is often the largest cost of all. Builders that let you bring your own model keys, as Sim does, let you pay the model provider directly instead of a resold rate. For a full cost model, see [how much it costs to build and run an AI agent](https://www.sim.ai/library/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-and-run-an-ai-agent).

## When is self-hosting the cheapest option?

Self-hosting is cheapest when someone on the team can run a server and the agent runs often enough that a hosted plan would move up its tiers. The license fee is zero for Sim's Apache 2.0 core, n8n's Community Edition, and Dify's Community Edition, but you still pay for:

- **A server.** Sizing depends on the workload, so test with your real agents.
- **Models.** Hosted model APIs bill per token, unless you run a local model.
- **Time.** Updates, backups, and security are your team's job.

For a side-by-side view of licenses and hosting, see [open-source AI agent platforms](https://www.sim.ai/library/open-source-ai-agent-platforms).

## How should a startup on a tiny budget choose?

1. **Write down one agent you need this month,** with its steps, its integrations, and how often it runs.
2. **Build it on a free plan or trial** and count how many credits, tasks, or executions one run uses.
3. **Multiply by a month of runs** and compare that against each vendor's lowest paid tier.
4. **Decide who runs it.** If nobody can maintain a server, pick a hosted plan even if self-hosting looks cheaper on paper.
5. **Ask about startup pricing** before paying list price.
