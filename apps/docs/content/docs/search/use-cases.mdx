Use these prompts as starting points on **Home**, in **Search** conversation mode. Replace the example names, dates, and sources with your own. The answer depends on what is connected, supported by the source, and accessible to you.

## Get up to speed

**Find a process and turn it into a checklist.**

> What does a new account owner need to do during their first week? Use our onboarding documentation and create a checklist with links to the instructions. Flag anything missing.

Follow up: “Which steps require someone else's approval?”

**Catch up after time away.**

> Summarize the Atlas project updates from October 1–14. Separate decisions, blockers, and next steps. Include owners only where the sources name them, and cite each item.

Follow up: “Which decisions changed an earlier plan?”

**Prepare for a meeting across apps.**

> Find my Atlas readiness review on November 16, 2026 in Google Calendar. Read the related Confluence project pages and Google Drive updates. Summarize what changed, which decisions are needed, and what I should read before the meeting, with citations.

Follow up: “Draft a five-point agenda based on the unresolved questions.” See [Prepare for a meeting](/search/ask-questions#prepare-for-a-meeting).

## Sales and customer success

**Prepare for an account meeting.**

> Find the available Acme account notes and implementation updates. Prepare a brief covering customer goals, commitments already made, unresolved questions, and a suggested agenda. Cite the sources.

Follow up: “Draft five discovery questions based on the unresolved topics.”

**Answer a product question.**

> Based on our current product documentation, how should I explain our SSO setup requirements to a customer? Draft a concise answer and include the supporting sources separately.

Follow up: “Which details need confirmation before I send this?”

## Engineering and product

**Understand why a decision was made.**

> Find the documented decision about the Atlas authentication approach. Summarize the options considered, the chosen approach, and its trade-offs. Distinguish the final decision from earlier proposals.

Follow up: “What assumptions would cause us to revisit the decision?”

**Compare feedback with a proposal.**

> Compare the customer feedback available for onboarding with the new onboarding proposal. Use a table showing each need, whether the proposal addresses it, and the supporting source. Mark gaps explicitly.

Follow up: “Turn the gaps into questions for the design review.”

## Support and IT

**Find a troubleshooting procedure.**

> Find our troubleshooting guidance for SSO sign-in failures. Summarize the recommended checks in order, cite each source, and identify when the guidance says to escalate.

Follow up: “Rewrite this as an internal support checklist.”

**Explain an access request.**

> How does an employee request access to our analytics tools? Find the documented request process, required approvals, and where to get help.

Follow up: “Draft a short answer for a new teammate.”

## Marketing and communications

**Write from approved messaging.**

> Use the approved Atlas launch brief to draft three announcement options for an internal audience. Keep each under 100 words and use only claims supported by the brief.

Follow up: “Combine the opening from option two with the structure of option three.”

**Reuse existing material.**

> Turn this product overview into an FAQ for customer-facing teams. Include a source for each answer and list questions the overview doesn't resolve.

Follow up: “Suggest an outline for a short enablement presentation.”

## People, operations, and leadership

**Understand a policy.**

> What does our travel policy say about hotels and meal reimbursement? Identify any regional differences and cite the policy sections. If the policy doesn't address a case, say so.

Follow up: “Create a short checklist for an employee booking their first trip.”

**Prepare a decision brief.**

> Compare these two vendor proposals using the requirements in our evaluation document. Organize the answer by requirement, cite the evidence, and distinguish missing information from a requirement the vendor does not meet.

Follow up: “List the questions we should ask before making a decision.”

## A prompt pattern you can reuse

> Using **[sources or topic]**, help me **[task]** for **[audience]**. Focus on **[scope and dates]**. Return **[format and length]**. Cite the sources and flag anything they don't establish.

You don't need every element for a simple question. Add them when the first answer is too broad or when the output will be shared.
